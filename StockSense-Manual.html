01

Getting started

Pick something to analyse, then read it in the right order.

🔎Search anything

Type a company, ticker, metal, currency or coin into the sidebar search — "gold", "usd inr", "bitcoin", "reliance". Quick-pick buttons cover the common ones.

📅Set the window

Choose how much history to load and, if you want, an end date in the past. Leave the end date empty to analyse today.

⏳Let it build

A progress bar runs while data is fetched and models are fitted. First load for a symbol is the slow one; after that it is cached.

Read in this order

AI snapshot for the summary → Scenario lab for the range of outcomes → Deep dive for the reasoning. Jumping straight to a trade suggestion skips the context that tells you how much to trust it.

02

Live vs Historical mode

One switch changes the meaning of everything on screen.

🟢Live mode

No end date set. The chart runs to today, the latest candle is still forming, and the headline price updates from a real-time quote. This is today's opinion.

🕰️Historical mode

You set an end date in the past. The system behaves as if that were today — no later data reaches any chart, indicator or model. This is a fair test of the method.

The separation is enforced in code rather than by habit: every price frame is tagged as current or historical, and the live-quote overlay refuses to touch a historical frame. The reason is simple — if a test of past performance can see even one future price, its results mean nothing.

Don't mix them

Never compare a historical-mode result against a live-mode one. They answer different questions. Use historical mode to ask "would this method have worked?" and live mode to ask "what does it say right now?"

Reading the scenario fan

Look at the width, not the middle line. A narrow fan means the method is confident. A wide one means it genuinely does not know — and knowing that is worth more than a confident-looking line that happens to be wrong.

04

Reading the numbers

The ones people misread most often.

Signal strength (α)

Every raw prediction is multiplied by a confidence factor between 0 and 1, fitted during validation. It is the single most important number on the dashboard.

α = 0No reliable edge found. The forecast is deliberately flattened to "no change".
α ≈ 0.3Weak edge. The prediction is heavily damped toward flat.
α ≈ 0.8Validated edge. The prediction passes through close to intact.
If the forecast line looks flat

That is almost always α = 0, and it is the system working correctly — not a bug. It has tested itself, found no dependable signal for this symbol at this horizon, and is refusing to guess. A flat line is an honest answer.

Model skill vs "no change"

The benchmark every forecast is measured against is simply predicting that the price will not move. Positive means the model beats that benchmark. Zero or negative means it does not, and the dashboard shows you that rather than hiding it.

Forecast horizons

Forecasts run at 1, 5, 10, 21 and 63 trading days — roughly a day, a week, a fortnight, a month and a quarter. Each is validated independently, so they can disagree. That disagreement is information.

05

The Deep dive tabs

Seven tabs, each answering a different question.

🧾Research Report

A written summary of the instrument — what it is, how it has behaved, what stands out right now.

🔮Predict a Date

Pick any future date and get the forecast for it, with a buy / sell / hold signal and the confidence behind it.

🕯️Candlestick Reading

Detected patterns, each shown with its measured reliability on this specific symbol rather than its textbook reputation. The two often disagree — trust the measurement.

🎯Best Buy/Sell Window

Scans the horizons for the window with the best expected move after trading costs are deducted.

📐Same-day plan

An intraday plan: entry level, stop loss and target for today's session.

📊Technical Analysis

The full indicator bank — trend, momentum, volatility, volume and structure, all in one place.

🧠Model Insights

How the model was built for this symbol and how well it actually scored in validation. The tab that tells you whether to believe the others.

06

IPO Center

A separate page for Indian mainboard and SME public issues.

🟢Live & upcoming

Grey market premium and live subscription figures by investor category for issues that are open or announced.

📊Recent listings

Past IPOs tracked from issue price through to listing day, with current price where the symbol can be matched.

🧪How reliable is GMP

Base rates from the listing record, plus an accuracy score for grey market premium that builds up over time.

What GMP is, and isn't

Grey market premium is what unofficial traders pay above the issue price before a stock lists. It is unregulated, unofficial and moves daily. It carries some information about listing day and essentially none about whether a business is worth owning afterwards.

Mean vs median

Listing gains have a long right tail — a handful of spectacular debuts drag the average far above the typical outcome. Plan around the median, not the mean. The dashboard shows both side by side for exactly this reason.

Why one panel fills in slowly

Measuring whether GMP was right needs two numbers per IPO: the premium quoted while it was still open, and the price it actually listed at. The second is published permanently. The first is not — the grey market board drops an IPO the moment it lists, and no source keeps a history of it. So the dashboard now saves the live board every time you open the page, and the accuracy score switches on once enough logged issues have listed. Opening the page regularly is what fills it.

07

The options plan

Index option selling, priced against real exchange data.

The options engine builds short straddle plans on NIFTY and BANKNIFTY and tests them against actual traded prices from the exchange's daily records, not theoretical values. Its settings — which expiries to trade and whether to use a stop — were chosen by comparing many configurations across a full year and keeping only what held up in two independent halves of that period.

Understand this risk before using it

Option selling produces many small wins and occasional large losses. The worst drawdown in testing exceeded the total profit earned. A long run of green days is the normal appearance of this strategy and tells you nothing about the size of the loss still to come.

08

What the system can't do

The honest section. Read it before acting on anything.

📉It can't reliably predict size

Forecasting how much a price will move is close to impossible at these horizons. Expect flat forecasts often — that is the model being honest.

🧭Direction is weak, not absent

Some asset classes lean slightly better than a coin flip. Others don't lean at all. The Model Insights tab tells you which case you're in.

🧾Backtests flatter reality

Tests exclude slippage, partial fills, and the very real difficulty of holding a losing position without closing it early.

📡Free data has gaps

Some feeds publish malformed or incomplete bars. The system detects these and disables the affected features rather than trusting them.

Not financial advice

This is an analysis tool. Every forecast carries uncertainty the dashboard shows you openly, and no output here is a recommendation to buy or sell. If a number looks too good to be true, treat it as a bug to investigate rather than an opportunity to act on.

The audit is the safety net

It checks that historical mode never sees future data, that live quotes never touch historical frames, and that every data source still returns what the code expects. Sources change without warning — a provider once moved a table to a different page and two panels silently went blank. Run the audit after any change; it exits with an error if something important breaks.

After editing the theme

Visual settings load once at startup. Refreshing the browser will not pick them up — stop the server and start it again.
